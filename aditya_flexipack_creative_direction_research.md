# Aditya Flexipack: Creative Direction Research ("Through the Press")

Research only. No code has been written. Prepared 2 Nov 2026.

This document replaces two assumptions in the earlier blueprints:

1. **The brand colour is red, not blue.** The logo was sampled pixel by pixel; there is no blue in it.
2. **The floating hero object is retired.** Bliss and Knurl both already use the "floating hero object that expands" and the "rolling ring", so this concept uses neither.

It brings together four strands of research:

- the client dossier, with every claim checked against a source;
- the competitor audit;
- a frame-by-frame study of the Dribbble references;
- an inventory of what Bliss and Knurl already use.

All four feed one original concept.

---

## 0. TL;DR

- **The idea.** The website is a press run. As you scroll, one printed film (the "web") travels through Aditya's own plant, station by station. It goes from polymer pellet to finished pouch to a shelf where one slot is left empty for the visitor's brand.
- **The hero.** A "3D wallpaper" in the spirit of the soft-drink shot the user picked:
  - The field is a sheet of **crimson metallised film** with soft crinkles, lit by the cursor.
  - A silver high-barrier pouch sits sandwiched between the lines of a giant condensed headline.
  - Indian snack and raw-material props float at different focus depths.
  - The whole hero is framed as a printer's proof sheet: crop marks, registration targets and a CMYK colour bar.
- **The packs travel.** This answers the brief that packs should float and shift into new positions.
  - The hero pouch **deflates and flattens into its own printed layflat**.
  - The layflat **peels apart into its layers**.
  - It rides the web through the plant and **comes off the line as pouches**.
  - At the end every pack **floats down and docks into a supermarket shelf**.
- **Palette from the logo.** Logo crimson #CE0000 (exact sample) and silver from the tagline. Together they are literally what Aditya makes: ink on metallised film. Plus press black and CMYK used only as print furniture.
- **Why it wins.** No Indian converter publishes barrier data, a structure library, EPR guidance, a sample-kit flow or verifiable certificate numbers. Aditya can do all five, wrapped in an experience no packaging site has.

## 3. Bliss and Knurl: what we must not repeat

Taken from the source of `D:/Bliss-Wedding` and `D:/Gym-Website`, and from the live knurl-gym.vercel.app bundle. Most recognisable first:

1. A ring-shaped 3D object in the hero that turns face-on, then the camera flies through its hole (both projects).
2. A `circle()` clip-path portal that opens out of the hero.
3. A ring or wheel rolling along the bottom of a pinned horizontal strip, with its label or colour changing per panel (both projects).
4. Pinned horizontal-pan sections (`containerAnimation`) with parallax panel images.
5. A floating object over a giant wordmark, with a gentle bob and pointer tilt.
6. Giant wordmark letters that part on scroll or rise out of the bottom edge.
7. A word-by-word opacity manifesto, and SplitText lines rising out of masks.
8. A counter loader (years or minutes) with a cover lift or a dot that scales up.
9. Custom cursors with Play, Drag or View labels.
10. One deliberate theme switch at the end of the page.
11. `inset()` clip-path image reveals with a scale-down inside the mask.
12. Masked bento grids.
13. Odometer digits and count-up stats.
14. A sticky left heading, or a sticky step number.
15. A `layoutId` morphing selector.
16. A marquee that speeds up with scroll.
17. Magnetic buttons and fill-from-bottom hovers.
18. A route-transition wipe.
19. Drag rails.
20. A hover list that swaps a sticky image.
21. 3D card tilt.
22. A circle-clip mobile menu.
23. A canvas grain overlay.
24. Palettes and type already used: red, wine, blush and gold with Jost (Bliss); onyx and gold with Cormorant (Idael); rubber and chalk with wide Archivo (Knurl).

The danger is that Aditya is red too, and Bliss is red, wine and blush. The answer is to pair crimson with **chrome silver, press black and CMYK** rather than with blush or gold. The result should read as industrial print, never as a wedding.

## 5. Competitor gaps we exploit

From the competitor audit: about 15 sites across Indian majors, North-India regionals and global benchmarks. Full sources are in section 12.

**Every competitor does these**, and they are what make a converter's site look generic:

- Unproven superlatives ("leading", "trusted").
- Carousels.
- Flat product grids with "Read more".
- Sustainability stated as adjectives.
- **Zero barrier data.** Not one Indian site publishes OTR/WVTR or layer structures.
- Unlabelled logo walls and certificate images.
- A generic contact form as the only conversion path.
- Machinery lists instead of buyer outcomes.
- Signs of neglect: lorem ipsum, "#" links, a © 2015 footer, typos (Shakoflex, GLS, Skypack, Paharpur, and Aditya itself).

**Nobody does these. Aditya will:**

1. **An India-specific structure library.** Namkeen, biscuits, atta, edible oil, spices, tea, detergent, pharma. For each one: a typical structure, its barrier class, which filling machine it suits (VFFS/HFFS), and its EPR category (II flexible vs III multilayer).
2. **An EPR-ready hub.** The recycled-content obligations, the brand-owner registration number or QR required on packs, and the food-contact exemptions. Constantia's PPWR hub is the model, applied to Indian rules. Check every date against the gazette.
3. **A mono-material migration path.** Today's structure shown beside a mono-PP or mono-PE alternative. Aditya's **Bobst AluBond** metallised CPP/BOPP is the hero product here. Recyclability claims need third-party validation first.
4. **A transparent process.** Artwork → cylinder → proof → machine trial → production, with honest lead-time and MOQ bands supplied by the client.
5. **A structured RFQ plus a sample-kit request.** Fields: format, dimensions, monthly volume band, current structure, filling machine, shelf-life target. Sample kits appear on no competitor site.
6. **Verifiable credentials.** Certificate number, scope, grade and validity, linked to the BRCGS Directory.
7. **Named, quantified stories** for Parle, ITC and Britannia. The awards are already public; permission to tell the stories is needed.
8. **The vertical-integration loop told visually:** inks, cylinders, film, metallising, conversion, recycling, pyrolysis.
9. **Kanpur/UP local search**, since local rivals are mostly only on IndiaMART.

Steal-worthy features from the global benchmarks:

- ProAmpac MAKR: a three-step configurator ending in a physical prototype.
- Mondi: a block per product with recyclability rating, PCR, HFFS/VFFS fit and number of colours.
- Constantia: good/better/best barrier tiers and before/after sliders.
- ePac: a two-track entry, "standard" or "custom".
- Cosmo Films: an MOQ-band field, WhatsApp, and published capacities.

## 6. The concept: "Through the Press"

**One-line idea:** scrolling the site *is* a press run. A single continuous printed film runs down the page through Aditya's own plant. Each section is a station that does something physical to it. The packs it becomes float off the line and, at the very end, dock into a retail shelf where one slot is empty and waiting.

**Why this is new:**

- It is built from the **physics of flexible packaging**: register, metallise, laminate, slit, deflate, peel, tear, seal.
- It does not rely on generic web motion such as reveal, parallax, pin or count-up.
- None of these moves appear on any site we reviewed, and none overlap the Bliss/Knurl list.

### 6.1 Page-wide systems (print furniture, used sparingly)

- **Colour-bar progress.**
  - A thin control strip along the top edge with one patch per station (C, M, Y, K, crimson, silver, and so on).
  - Each patch inks in as you reach its station.
  - It replaces a generic progress bar and is hidden under 640 px.
- **Eye-mark index.** Eye-marks are the small black rectangles printed on real film that packing machines read with a photocell.
  - A column of them on the right edge is the section index; each one can be clicked or focused.
  - As the visitor passes a mark, a tiny sensor tick reads it.
- **Registration targets and crop marks** appear only where the page is genuinely a "sheet": the hero frame and the die-line drawing. Used anywhere else they become decoration, which the Taste skill calls "costume".
- **No custom cursor, grain overlay, marquee or magnetic buttons.** The restraint is part of what makes it distinctive.

### 6.2 Motion language

Mechanical and material, never floaty.

| Verb | Meaning on the page | Replaces (Bliss/Knurl device) |
|---|---|---|
| **Register** | Separated CMYK plates snap into alignment | Counter loader |
| **Deflate** | A pouch exhales and lies flat | Floating object that expands |
| **Peel** | A corner lifts and a layer curls away | `inset()` clip reveals |
| **Slit** | Knife lines run down and lanes separate | Bento grid |
| **Tear** | A zig-zag notch rips along the section edge | `circle()` portal |
| **Crimp** | Heat-seal serrations stamp a closure | Fill-from-bottom button hover |
| **Code** | Dot-matrix inkjet prints a batch code dot by dot | Odometer and count-up |
| **Dock** | A pack settles into a slot with real weight | Hover list that swaps a sticky image |

**Easing.** Use roller-like motion: constant velocity, a short accelerate and decelerate, and a slight tension overshoot only on "snap" verbs. Avoid expo.out on everything.

**Scrubbing.** Where an animation is scrubbed by scroll, it moves `linear`, one to one with the scroll (Hallmark's rule).

## 7. Section-by-section choreography (homepage)

The sections are stations along the web. The verified facts are taken from the client dossier (section 1).

### Station 00: Hero, "The Proof Sheet" (the 3D wallpaper)

- **Frame.**
  - The hero is a sheet inset 16–24 px from the viewport, with square corners: printed sheets are square, and this is also the Taste default.
  - Outside it sit hairline crop marks at the four corners, registration targets in the middle of each side, and the **colour control bar** along the bottom edge.
- **Field.** A **crimson metallised film with soft, large crinkles**: a real-time plane carrying a normal map, with anisotropic metal and clearcoat.
  - **The cursor is the light**: moving it rakes a specular highlight across the crinkles. We do not tilt an object.
  - On touch devices the light drifts slowly on its own, or follows the gyroscope where permitted.
  - This is Aditya's own product (coloured metallised film), in its own colour, used as wallpaper.
- **Headline.**
  - Giant condensed type of **seven words or fewer**, in the near-black crimson ink #1E0003. That passes 3:1 on #CE0000; the maths is in 8.3.
  - Copy options (the client chooses):
    - **"We make the part you tear open."**
    - "Microns thick. Kilometres long."
    - "The skin of India's snacks."
- **Product.** One real-time 3D stand-up pouch in **high-barrier silver** (an AluBond-style metallised look), printed with Aditya's own "house" artwork: the AF monogram and crimson registration marks. Never a client's brand without written permission.
  - It sits **between** headline lines 1 and 2, exactly as the soft-drink reference does.
  - Its foil reflects the crimson field around it.
- **Props, with depth of field and still** (no bobbing). Placed like a wallpaper composition at three depths:
  - near and blurred;
  - mid and sharp;
  - far and blurred.

  Two families:

  - Indian FMCG props: bhujia strands, a plain biscuit, a red chilli, cardamom, tea leaves, a potato chip.
  - Raw-material props: three translucent resin pellets, a pencil-sized gravure cylinder, a crimson ink droplet, a slit reel.

  The cursor moves the light and the near props shift by a few pixels, a depth cue rather than a tilt.
- **One quiet glass tag** on the pouch carrying a real spec line, for example "PET 12 µm / Met-CPP 25 µm / PE 40 µm".
  - The values must be confirmed with Aditya's technical team.
- **Navigation:** AF monogram, four plain links, and one action, "Request a sample kit".
- **First-load entrance: "Plates register."**
  - The sheet prints in quick passes: cyan, magenta, yellow, black, then crimson spot, then silver.
  - Each plate is slightly out of register and snaps into alignment while the registration targets converge.
  - It takes 900–1200 ms, then one light sweep runs across the foil.
  - Return visits and reduced-motion users see the finished sheet.
- **Exception to record.** Product-over-headline breaks the Taste rule "nothing on top of a headline". We waive it on the record, because the user named this reference. The legibility rule: the pouch covers at most about 25% of any one line, and never the line's key word.

### Station 01: "Every pack starts flat" (deflate → layflat)

- **The first "float and shift" moment.**
  - On scroll, the hero pouch **exhales**: its inflation goes to zero, its bottom gusset unfolds, and it **lies flat as its printed layflat**.
  - The layflat rotates to face the camera and **docks into a die-line drawing** that shows dimension lines, seal zones (hatched), the tear notch, the zipper track and the eye-mark.
- **Meanwhile the field changes** from crimson foil to a cool drawing sheet: the crinkles iron out as the normal-map strength falls to zero.
- **Copy:** one sentence. The pouch is a print before it's a pack, and a polymer before it's a print.
- **Why it matters for B2B:** brand managers instantly see that Aditya thinks in layflats, repeats and seals.

### Station 02: "The Laminate, built, not exploded" (peel + composer)

- **Peel.** The layflat's corner lifts and it **peels** into its layers, which curl away and settle as a stack.
- **The composer.** Visitors choose a structure and watch it **laminate under a nip-roller line**, one layer at a time: print film → ink → adhesive → metallised barrier → sealant.
  - Each layer carries a callout with its µm value and its job.
  - The callout pattern comes from the Zeezoy shot; the layer-stack pattern from BioForge, inverted.
- **Crisp-o-meter.** One potato chip beside the stack. With too little barrier it visibly dulls and softens as a moisture indicator rises; as barrier layers are added it stays crisp.
  - Aditya listed a **Mocon WVTR/OTR lab** in its 2016 deck, so real measured values for 3–5 signature structures are a realistic request. Until they arrive, show indicative bands only.
- **Toggle:** Standard high-barrier / **Mono-PP with AluBond Met-CPP** / Mono-PE. Each shows its EPR category and its recyclability note (subject to validation).

### Station 03: "The Web Path" (the plant, in-house)

- **The spine of the site.** The layflat becomes a ribbon of film that **snakes down the page in S-wraps around idler rollers**, the way a real press web path runs.
  - The printed texture moves along the path as you scroll.
  - Each roller is a station, and something physical happens to the film at each one.
  - Show only the stations the dossier verifies.

| Station | What happens on screen | Fact to anchor (dossier, section 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Extrude | Pellets pour in and a tall translucent **blown-film bubble** rises, then collapses flat at the nip | 3/5/7-layer blown lines and CPP lines (site); the MAGNATE-1900 tower photo |
| Metallise | A silver vapour front sweeps across the film; Zajno's material-wave shader | **Bobst Vision K5 1,650 mm with AluBond, first in India (2020, press-verified)** |
| Engrave | Macro view of a copper gravure cylinder: cells fill with crimson ink and the doctor blade wipes | In-house prepress, engraving and re-engraving (site, photos) |
| Ink | A crimson droplet beside a lab-callout sheet | PU / NTNK / ARSR / water-based from the sister concern (likely Irisinks; confirm) |
| Print | CMYK and crimson plates register onto the film, echoing the hero intro at small scale | 8 rotogravure presses with Quadtec registration (2016 deck; re-confirm) |
| Laminate | A second web arrives and the two marry at the nip | Solventless, dry/wet and extrusion-coating lines (2016 deck; re-confirm) |
| Slit | Knife lines split the web; see station 04 | 13 slitters (2016 deck; re-confirm) |
| Pouch | Lanes fold, seal and cut into pouches that **lift off the line** | Centre, side and 3-side seal; stand-up; shaped; liquid (site) |

- **Plant stats print as inkjet batch codes.** In the Doto dot-matrix face, on the web's edge trim, as real multi-line coding: for example "SITE  RANIA, KANPUR DEHAT", "SOLAR 1.35 MW" and the acreage.
  - Print only figures the client confirms. Never use count-ups, and never join meta with middle dots (a Hallmark rule).

### Station 04: "Slit to order" (product range)

- One wide master reel. **Knife lines run down** it and it **splits into lanes**, one per category from the current site: Laminates, Pouches, Films, Inks, Cylinders, Polybags.
- Selecting a lane (by tap, click or keyboard) **unwinds** it to show formats, typical structures and links.
- This replaces the category card grid that every competitor uses.
- **On mobile** the lanes become stacked strips; tapping one unwinds it.

### Station 05: "One pack, many appetites" (applications / structure library)

- This is the KMBCH idea transformed. One pack stays centred while **its format morphs** (pillow → stand-up → side-gusset → spouted) and the **whole field and props change per Indian application**:

| Application | Field and props |
|---|---|
| Namkeen | Turmeric field, bhujia |
| Biscuits | Wheat field |
| Atta and rice | Bulk gusset |
| Spices | Masala red |
| Tea and coffee | Leaf and bean |
| Edible oil | Spout |
| Home care | Detergent |
| Pet food | Kibble; tells the US export story (Panjiva: Choice Pet Foods) |
| Laddi packs | A strip of sachets; a very Indian format no global site shows |

- Each state shows the **India structure-library card**: typical structure, barrier class, VFFS/HFFS fit, EPR category.
  - This content is the moat (gap #1).
- It is a pinned chapter, the site's one deliberately pinned scrub, with a static end state.

### Station 06: "Under the light table" (proof and credentials)

- Certificates and awards lie on a **back-lit QC inspection table**: BRC/BRCGS, ISO, HACCP, the Parle Gold supplier awards, ITC Foods and the Britannia appreciation.
- A **linen-tester loupe** (a printer's magnifier) appears over the table on hover or focus to read the certificate number, scope, grade and validity, with a link to the BRCGS Directory.
  - The loupe is a tool inside this one section, not a site-wide cursor.
- **Client stories** appear only with permission. The awards themselves are public.
- **Rule:** no invented numbers, no unlabelled logo wall.

### Station 07: "The loop closes" (sustainability and EPR)

- **Lifecycle transformation** (after Zajno's recycling piece, in our materials). One used pack: shelf → collection → shredded flake → recycled granule → pyrolysis oil.
  - The crimson pen draws the connecting path in **proof-correction marks**, the CU Mehrweg idea.
- **Translucent film cards** float over a photograph of the 1.35 MW rooftop solar array. This is the CU Mehrweg "frosted cards over sky" idea, in our material.
  - The cards also cover the three rainwater-harvesting systems and post-industrial recycled content (verify).
- **The EPR-ready hub entry** sits in the pack's own **QR code** (QR or registration numbers are mandated on multilayer packs). Opening it reveals the EPR guide.
- **Caveat:** the d2w (oxo-biodegradable) certification sits awkwardly beside a recyclability story. Discuss with the client before featuring it.

### Station 08: "Proof to pallet" (process and RFQ)

- **Process.** The steps arrive as **proof sheets stacking**, each receiving a rubber-stamp approval:
  - "Artwork received"
  - "Cylinder engraved"
  - "Proof approved"
  - "Machine trial passed"
  - "Dispatched"

  Indicative lead times and MOQ bands come from the client.
- **The RFQ / sample-kit request is a job ticket.** As the visitor fills in format, size, volume band, current structure, filling machine and shelf-life target, a printed **spec sheet assembles beside the form**.
  - On submit, the ticket **folds and heat-seals (crimp)** into a sachet.
- **Two tracks** (after ePac): "I have a structure, quote me" and "Help me choose a structure".

### Station 09: Finale, "The Shelf"

- **The last "float and shift."** Every pack that came off the line **drops from above and docks into a retail shelf**, with real weight and a small settle, each into its slot under shelf price-tag rails. The shelf can read as a kirana store or modern trade.
- **One slot is empty**, drawn as a dashed die-line: **"This one is for your brand."**
  - It is the final call to action, a sample kit or RFQ.
- **The footer** sits under the shelf: plant address, map, phone, WhatsApp and email. All are from the dossier and confirmed with the client.

### Section transitions

- **Tear:** for the one moment that needs drama, out of station 03. The film tears along a zig-zag tear notch, and the edge shows the stress-whitening real PE film shows when torn.
- **Crimp:** a single heat-seal serration line between stations 07 and 08.
- **Everywhere else:** plain cuts with a field-colour change. Nothing fades into anything (Taste).

### 8.2 Type (subject to Hallmark's `theme.mjs check --face`)

- **Display:** **Teko** or **Khand**, both condensed heavy faces by the Indian Type Foundry.
  - Both include **Devanagari**, so Hindi touches such as "कानपुर" work natively.
  - Test both on the real headline; Antonio is the fallback.
- **Body:** **Hanken Grotesk** (or Fontshare Switzer), 16–18 px, with tabular figures for spec tables.
- **Outlier, one role only:** **Doto**, a dot-matrix face, used only for the inkjet batch codes.
- **Avoid as defaults:** Inter, Roboto, Poppins, DM Sans and the other names on Hallmark's list, plus Jost, Archivo and Cormorant (all used before).

### 8.3 Accessibility maths (checked)

| Pair | Contrast | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| #CE0000 with white | ≈ 5.8:1 | Passes AA for text and buttons, in both directions |
| #1E0003 on #CE0000 | ≈ 3.4:1 | Passes for large display text only |
| #2B0004 on #CE0000 | ≈ 3.3:1 | Passes, but has less margin |
| Any lighter tonal crimson (e.g. #5E0006) | < 3:1 | Fails; never use it for the headline |

- Touch targets are at least 44 px.
- Every interaction is keyboard-operable (lanes, the loupe, the composer, the eye-mark index).

## 9. Technical feasibility (for the build phase, not now)

- **Stack.** Next.js 16 (already in the repo; read `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` first, per AGENTS.md), Tailwind 4, three.js with React Three Fiber.
  - For scroll: GSAP ScrollTrigger, or native `animation-timeline` for the simple scrubs.
- **Hero foil.** One plane using MeshPhysicalMaterial (metalness 1, anisotropy, clearcoat) with a baked crinkle normal map. The cursor drives a point or area light. Cheap.
- **Hero pouch.** A procedural pillow made from a displaced subdivided plane with an "inflation" uniform. That same uniform drives the station 01 deflate, so there is no second model.
  - Gussets and seals are geometry strips. The print is a texture atlas.
  - Optionally upgrade to a scanned or modelled GLB (under 1.5 MB, Draco/Meshopt) if the client supplies a real sample.
- **Props.** Pre-rendered (Blender) as transparent AVIF/WebP layers at three depths, with CSS or WebGL depth-of-field.
  - This is "3D-looking" at near-zero runtime cost. Only the pouch is real-time.
- **Web path.**
  - The path itself: an SVG path for layout, with a WebGL ribbon (or a CSS-masked repeating texture) whose UV offset is scroll-driven.
  - Rollers are simple cylinders.
  - **Mobile:** a straight vertical web.
- **Heavy scenes on low-end and mobile** (pellets, metallising wave, gravure macro) become scroll-scrubbed image sequences or short muted videos.
- **Performance budget:** hero LCP under 2.5 s on 4G, the first WebGL frame after LCP, and the JS for 3D lazy-loaded per station.
- **Fallbacks:** no WebGL → the static composited hero image; reduced motion → final states; no JS → full content.
